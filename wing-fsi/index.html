1-Way Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) Parametric Optimization of a High-Downforce Racing Wing

Project Overview & Engineering Objectives

In high-performance vehicle dynamics, aerodynamic surfaces must generate substantial downforce while maintaining structural integrity under extreme aerodynamic loads. Elastic deformation of an airfoil alters its effective angle of attack, leading to unpredictable aerodynamic effects.

This project demonstrates a 1-Way Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) computational pipeline. By fully coupling a 3D steady-state CFD simulation to a linear structural model, the structural response of a solid Carbon Fiber racing wing section was evaluated across a operating range of 20 m/s to 55 m/s.

Key Technical Focus Areas:

  • Aerodynamic Characterization: Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) mapping of NACA 4412 of size 250x500
  • 1-Way Load Interpolation: Surface mesh mapping to accurately transfer fluid pressure matrices to structural elements.
  • Parametric Optimization: Automated multi-design-point sweeping to evaluate structural scaling under aerodynamic force.

Computational Workflow Architecture

To execute the coupled simulation, a dual-domain pipeline was constructed inside Ansys Workbench, linking a finite volume fluid solver directly to a finite element structural solver.

Aerodynamic Performance Analysis (CFD)

Fluid Domain Setup:

  • Solver: Pressure-Based Finite Volume Solver.
  • Turbulence Modeling: Enhanced turbulence solver to capture adverse pressure gradients along the aft portion of the chord.
  • Mesh Resolution: 880,000-cell fluid enclosure featuring a refined wake inflation layer to resolve vortex shedding trailing edge characteristics.

Mesh Discretization: Domain Sizing and Inflation Strategy

The accuracy of this FSI analysis relied heavily on a well-meshed grid across both physics domains. For the aerodynamic domain, a finite volume mesh of approximately 880,000 cells was generated within the fluid enclosure. Global element sizing was tightly managed, with a localized body sizing constraint applied around the airfoil to capture high-velocity gradients. A structured boundary layer inflation profile was embedded along the active wing skin, utilizing 10 layers with a 1.25 geometric growth rate to guarantee tight boundary-layer resolution and accurate suction-surface pressure mapping.

For the structural simulation, hex-dominant mesh was selected to handle the solid body volume. The element sizing was parametrically altered, transitioning from an initial fine 4 mm grid down to a relatively bigger 12 mm grid. This strategic sizing shift drastically reduced the node count to fit comfortably within transfer licensing limits {student license} while perfectly preserving the geometric curvature of the nose and capturing the peak bending stress gradients at the cantilevered root joint.

Velocity Field Profile:

As fluid approaches the leading edge, it splits, forcing high-velocity flow lines along the lower camber of the wing profile. This local velocity acceleration generates a massive localized drop in static pressure according to Bernoulli’s principle.

  • Imported Aerodynamic Load: The 3D continuous pressure matrix from the fluid wall boundary was successfully interpolated across the mechanical surface elements.

Comprehensive Engineering Insights

1. Quadratic Force Translation

The aerodynamic downforce vector scales quadratically relative to velocity according to the standard relationship: \(F_d = \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 C_d A\)

Because the solid carbon fiber matrix remains within its linear elastic region, the mechanical simulation matches this behavior perfectly. When the vehicle speed climbs from 20 m/s to 55 m/s (a 2.75x increase), the max internal stress increases by a factor of 7.48x (0.114 MPa to 0.853 MPa), matching the predictable 2.75² exponential scaling.

2. High-Performance Structural Stiffness

At the absolute peak operating speed of 55 m/s (~200 km/h or 123 mph), the wing element experiences a maximum structural deflection of only 0.125 mm at the outboard tip. This tiny deflection value confirms that a solid carbon fiber layout delivers immense structural rigidity, preventing any adverse twisting that could cause aerodynamic stalling.

3. Ultimate Safety Factor Evaluation

The peak equivalent stress observed under full velocity loading is 0.853 MPa. High-quality motorsport-grade carbon fiber laminates typically boast an ultimate tensile strength exceeding 500–1000 MPa. This confirms that this solid geometry baseline operates with an exceptionally conservative factor of safety, opening up substantial opportunities to optimize the wing through topological lightweighting or shell hollow-coring in future designs.

Data Pipeline Troubleshooting & Management

A critical component of this engineering study involved managing the shared geometry upstream data pipeline across conflicting physics solvers:

  • The Challenge: Activating the solid physics parameters for FEA mechanical meshing introduced an unhandled boundary intersecting the background fluid enclosure mesh, causing a global downstream data invalidation error across the design points.
  • The Resolution: Implemented selective Mesh Component Suppression Filters within the separate solvers. The fluid solver was instructed to mesh only the hollow enclosure cavity while suppressing the solid wing body. Conversely, the structural solver was locked to mesh only the solid body volume while suppressing the enclosure.
  • Future Best Practice: To fully decouple upstream CAD modifications from downstream sweeps, future iterations will implement boundary-face Named Selections directly within the geometry interface. This isolates the data-transfer pipeline and allows for quick material or mesh updates without triggering full fluid domain recalculations.
